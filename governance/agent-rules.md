# Agent governance

Thirty agents with access to ad accounts, websites, a CRM and Slack can do real damage. These are the rules every agent in The Night Shift runs under.

## What agents can do alone

- Optimize keywords, search terms and negatives in Google Ads
- Fix SEO issues on the five sites after a preview is approved
- Draft newsletters, design pieces and replies to colleagues
- Report what they did, in their own channel

## What always comes to me

- **Money.** Any budget change, in any channel. Budgets follow a ramp plan set by leadership.
- **Anything that goes to another person.** Colleagues never get an agent's work without my approval.
- **Deleting.** No agent deletes a service page, a campaign or a contact.
- **Inventing.** No agent creates a new service, claim or number that doesn't already exist.

## How agents must report

- **Confirm before you report.** An agent opens the page or tool and checks the problem is real. If it can't confirm, it doesn't report it.
- **Say the whole thing.** An approval request explains what needs approving in full. No codes I have to look up.
- **Post only in your channel.** Ads in one channel, organic in another, approvals in a third, one daily recap.

## How I can approve

From my phone, in one tap, in one channel. If approving takes me more than a minute, the request was written wrong.

## Why this matters

Most AI horror stories in marketing come from agents with no limits, not from bad models. Governance is what lets me sleep while they work.
